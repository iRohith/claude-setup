# New visual work: create or replace the visual world

The branch of [new-work.md](new-work.md) step 3 for a new or replacement world. Steps 4 to 7 of new-work.md follow it.

### Create or replace the visual world

1. Name the product's unique mechanism in one sentence, the audience's real scene, its cultural home, and what this first surface must prove. Note the page this category always ships and its predictable opposite; both are the rut, kept out of the seven-candidate list. A brief that paints its own picture, a product name, a titled artifact, a governing metaphor, adds its literal reading to the rut: spend at most one candidate on it and derive the rest from elsewhere in the audience's world.
2. From that cultural world, list seven concrete visual systems, artifacts, places, or rituals the audience knows by heart, each with one line on why it resonates and can carry the mechanism and one on how it looks (its ground and main colours, its type voices, the one signature that makes it recognisable), ordered by resonance. The audience's world includes its graphic and screen traditions, not only its physical objects: the notation, publications, identity programs, data graphics, and interfaces it reads daily. On an Operate or Read surface, the seven still come from anywhere in that world (its graphic traditions, places, rituals, and artifacts), never from the tools and instruments the audience operates: a terminal, a scope, or a control panel handed to a working screen comes back as a costume of that tool. A nameable abstract system (a school of poster, a documentation standard) is as concrete a candidate as any artifact. On a Persuade or Experience surface, the tools and instruments the audience operates count toward the rut: spend at most one candidate on them. Also ask what this thing would look like as a physical object and what its world looked like before the web. Near-duplicates count once. When more than three of the seven share one material family, the derivation stopped at the subject's most obvious artifact; dig until the list spans at least three families.
3. Turn that material into complete directions: each joins a reusable visual world to a concrete first-surface experience.
4. Weigh the directions yourself: every run converging on the category default is the failure this step prevents. Rank the directions from step 3 by resonance, then take the top one as the lead and test it against the rest on exactly two axes, audience identification and product clarity. A candidate that beats the lead on both becomes the lead; one that holds one axis stays a full alternate; one that loses both is declined. A declined candidate is not spent: name the one discipline of its system the lead lacks (a palette's total commitment, a grid's density courage, a form's structural honesty), raise the lead to match, and write each raise into the presented direction as its own line, named for its donor; a raise nobody can read did not happen. A donation transfers ambition and system discipline, never clothes: a component treatment, such as a button's shadow or a display face, is clothes, and one world owns the page. The one exception is a direction the user or brief pins whole, naming its world and its first-surface experience both in words you can quote: then there is nothing to derive or present; record the pin under section 5. Anything less goes through this step. <!-- rule:skill-concept-procedure --> <!-- rule:skill-verdict-and-donation -->
5. Present the directions with the AskUserQuestion tool: the lead, fully committed and already raised, then at most one more full alternate (your strongest competitive candidate), then the category standard played straight, last (the standing exit below). Each option's description carries the same anatomy: thesis, palette (ground, main colours and what each does), type voices, first viewport, signature interaction, and an honest risk, naming its familiarity when true. Declined candidates fold into the lead's description as the lines it kept from them. The owner picks one, or asks for a fresh set: a fresh set drops every direction already shown, and after two fresh sets in a row, ask what quality is missing. Change the lead on your own only on named factual grounds, when it cannot carry the product's truth or task; taste is never grounds. If the owner is away, build the lead, record it with `wf assume`, and carry on. <!-- rule:skill-pick-card-one-only -->

The standing exit: every direction round offers one quiet, permanent alternative, the category standard, played straight. It is the user's door, never yours: never recommend it, never weigh it against the lead, never let it soften the other directions; the counterweights bind the unchosen default, not the chosen one. When the user takes it (that option, or plain words asking for the familiar or competitor-like path), convention becomes the commitment: ask once for two or three products this should sit alongside, make their craft level the bar, and execute the canon at full fidelity, without irony or smuggled quirk. Record a standing preference as a brand commitment in PRODUCT.md. <!-- rule:skill-canon-standing-exit --> A user- or brief-pinned direction beats your ranking, always. A pin names the look itself, in the user's words or as a brand commitment the user made binding; a product the brief cites as a reference sets the craft bar, not the world, and an answer to an open question that restates the brief pins nothing. Resolve collisions field by field: preserve every user- or brief-pinned constraint, and when only a direction's materials conflict with a pinned visual direction or a PRODUCT.md brand commitment, translate that material expression and name the translation in the presented direction. <!-- rule:skill-assigned-plus-reroll -->

Every direction you present must already be viable: every relationship and claim it visualizes true, a real palette and component family, a distinctive composition with one product-specific experience, workable at full-surface scale within the available assets, tools, and performance budget. A candidate that fails on truth is replaced before the round, never rescued by it. Truth binds claims, not demonstrations: in greenfield work, author whatever illustrative material the concept needs at full fidelity, label it synthetic wherever a visitor could mistake it for the real thing, and hand the user the list of what to replace with real material. What stays uninventable are commercial and factual claims: prices, customers, benchmarks, endpoints, capabilities the product does not have. Refusing a bold direction because its demonstration data does not exist yet is the timidity reflex wearing honesty's clothes. <!-- rule:skill-truth-binds-claims -->

What the opening must do, and how far the world may reach into the page, depends on the surface's mode. Read the mode's rules before writing the directions, and again before judging the build: [mode-persuade.md](mode-persuade.md) for Persuade and Experience, [mode-operate.md](mode-operate.md), or [mode-read.md](mode-read.md). Where they conflict with shared guidance, they win.
